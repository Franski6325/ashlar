01 — The atelier

Two rooms, one argument: slow is a specification.

Everything happens between the vat room and the press floor, eleven metres apart. A sheet is wet on Monday and printed the following week — we have never found a way to shorten that which we liked.

01

The vat & the mould

Cotton half-stuff is beaten for six hours in a Hollander until the fibre draws like cream. Each sheet is lifted on a laid mould, couched onto woollen felts, pressed at forty tonnes and hung to cure for a fortnight.

04 — The bindery

Four motions make a book.

Scroll to walk a signature from flat sheet to cased volume. Nothing here is automated; the thread is linen and the paste is wheat starch.

  1. 01
    Fold

    Each sheet is folded with the grain and bone-creased once, never twice.

  2. 02
    Gather

    Folded sections are nested in order and knocked square against the bench.

  3. 03
    Sew

    Linen thread through five stations, kettle-stitched at head and tail.

  4. 04
    Case

    Boards are covered, the block is cased in, and the title is struck in brass.

06 — The bench

What we believe, and what is happening right now.

The floor keeps a running book: every pull, proof and sewing is logged by hand and mirrored here. Conditions matter — paper moves with the room.

Principle 01

Grain before gloss.

The direction of the fibre decides how a book opens, how a fold behaves, and whether a spine will last a century. We choose it first and design around it.

Principle 02

Impression is an argument.

Too light and the type floats; too deep and the sheet is punished. We set it by feel, in front of the press, with the paper we will actually run.

Principle 03

Make fewer. Make them last.

Every sheet is alkaline-buffered and tub-sized to ISO 9706. If a copy is still legible in 2226, the run was worth pulling.

    07 — Capability

    Everything a sheet can be put through here.

    Open a line to see the board change. If what you need is not listed, it probably still exists somewhere in the building — ask.

    Cotton, flax and abaca beaten in-house and pulled on laid or wove moulds up to 640 × 900 mm. Bespoke wire watermarks are sewn to the mould for runs over one hundred sheets, and every sheet is tub-sized in gelatine for a surface that takes both letterpress and dip pen.

    A 1954 Heidelberg platen for the runs and a Vandercook for the proofs. Photopolymer plates for contemporary type, foundry metal for the faces that deserve it. Rubber-base inks mixed on a slab and matched to a drawdown you approve before we run.
